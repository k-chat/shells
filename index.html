01 THE RULE

All the shells
in the world
are the same shell.

Every one is a tube that grows only at its mouth. The old part no longer changes.

A snail, a nautilus, a clam, a ram's horn, an elephant's tusk. One rule — and three numbers.

SCROLL TO CHANGE THE NUMBERS
DAVID M. RAUP · 1966 A SCROLL ESSAY

02 T · THE DROP

The tower
lies down.

The third number is how far the tube drops with each turn. Give it height and the coil stacks into a tower — the sea snail.

Take the drop to zero and every whorl stays in one plane. The same spiral closes into a nautilus.

T  2.10 → 0.00  ·  W 3.0  ·  D 0.00

03 W · THE WIDENING

A lid before
the first turn.

The first number is how much the tube widens with each turn. A nautilus multiplies by three.

Make it a million and the tube cannot finish one revolution. It flattens into a valve — a clam. Next to the hinge, a tiny coil remains where the spiral began.

W  3.00 → 1,000,000  ·  D  0.00 → 0.03  ·  T  0.00

04 D · THE DISTANCE

A hole
in the middle.

The second number is how far from the centre the tube turns. Close in, each whorl covers the last.

Push the tube outward and the turns separate, leaving an empty axis. The shell becomes a ram's horn.

W  1,000,000 → 7.0  ·  D  0.03 → 0.38  ·  T  0.00 → 0.60

05 OUTSIDE THE SHELL

Not a shell.
Still the rule.

Push the distance far out and let the tube widen fast. No whorl ever closes.

The tube stretches into one long, open curve — an elephant's tusk: a horn that never becomes a house. The same rule that built the snail built this.

W  7.0 → 1,585  ·  D  0.38 → 0.86  ·  T  0.60 → 0.00

06 THE CUBE

Almost every
shell is missing.

In 1966, David Raup gave three numbers to a computer. It drew shells that looked real without copying any. Then he arranged every possible shell in a cube: each axis is one of the numbers, each point is a shell — including shells no animal has ever had.

Raup thought the empty forms were possible on paper but impossible or inefficient for an animal. Stephen Jay Gould thought evolution simply never gave them a turn. Either way, life occupies only a few corners.

07 LIFE'S FEW CORNERS

Most forms
never lived.

Raup marked the forms found in living and fossil shells. Almost the whole cube stayed empty.

The five familiar forms hold their places. The unobserved possibilities shrink to points: life occupies only a few corners of the morphospace.

08 THE BENCH

With three numbers,
any shell.

Move the numbers and the tube redraws itself. Every point in the cube is a form; almost none of them have been lived in.

Start with the five forms from the story, then wander into the empty parts of the cube. Some of these shells never existed.
